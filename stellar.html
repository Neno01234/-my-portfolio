How Stars Die

Fusion holds gravity at bay. When the fuel runs out, gravity wins — and mass picks the ending.

2. Core Collapses

Three Endings

⚪ White Dwarf

≲ 8 M☉

Earth-sized ember. Electron pressure holds it up. Our Sun’s future.

Cap ≈ 1.4 M☉

🟠 Neutron Star

~8–25 M☉

City-sized, nuclear density. Born in a supernova. Often a pulsar.

Cap ≈ 2–2.5 M☉

🔴 Black Hole

≳ 25 M☉

Gravity wins completely. Light can’t escape the horizon.

Rs ≈ 3 km × (M/M☉)

Key Physics

Electron Degeneracy

Quantum electron pressure props up white dwarfs. Over ~1.4 M☉ → collapse.

Neutron Degeneracy

Nuclear-density neutrons hold neutron stars. Over ~2–2.5 M☉ → black hole.

Event Horizon

Cross this line — not even light returns.

Rs ≈ 3 (M / M☉) km

Photon Sphere

At 1.5× the horizon, light can skim unstable orbits — the bright ring in BH images.

rphoton = 1.5 Rs

Simulator Rules

  • < 8 M☉ → white dwarf
  • 8–25 M☉ → neutron star
  • > 25 M☉ → stellar black hole
  • Supermassive BH mode → galactic nucleus (~106–109 M☉)

Keep in Mind

  • Thresholds are simplified for learning.
  • Real stars also depend on rotation, winds, and companions.
  • This is a demo — not a research code.
